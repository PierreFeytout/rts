# Terrain surfaces

Generated by `scripts/generate-terrain.mjs` and committed. What each surface
is meant to *be* is in UNIVERSE.md — "Surfaces" for Furnace Nine, "Other
fronts" for anything after it. This file is the format.

```
node scripts/generate-terrain.mjs                 everything, about 40 s
node scripts/generate-terrain.mjs deepfreeze      one biome
node scripts/generate-terrain.mjs ashworks:ember  one surface
```

Deterministic: regenerating something that has not changed leaves the
repository untouched, so a diff here always means somebody changed a number.

## The files

Three per surface, named `<biome>_<surface>_<map>.png`.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `_albedo` | Colour, sRGB, 512×512. Carries its own cavity shading — see below. |
| `_normal` | Tangent-space normal in RGB (OpenGL convention, green up), **the surface's height in alpha**, 512×512. |
| `_orm` | Occlusion, roughness, metalness — the glTF channel order three.js reads `aoMap`, `roughnessMap` and `metalnessMap` from — and **glow in alpha**, 256×256. |

The alpha channels are data, not transparency. A viewer will show these two
files as partly see-through; nothing is wrong. Height is what the blend
between two painted surfaces follows, and glow is what the surface emits.

`terrain.json` is the manifest: per surface, how many world tiles one repeat
covers, how brightly its glow emits, and its average colour — which is what
the minimap draws, so it cannot drift out of step with the ground it is a
picture of. The generator measures it rather than anyone typing it.

## Why the colour carries its own shading

The camera is far enough away that a normal map alone cannot sell relief: at
twenty screen pixels per tile a crack is two pixels, and the light across two
pixels is not readable as a groove. So every surface bakes a cavity term into
its albedo — crevices darker, convex edges lighter. That is what a painter
does, and it is why painted terrain reads as more detailed than lit terrain
at the same resolution. The normals still matter: they are what makes the
same surface look different under Furnace Nine's low warm key and Cistern
Four's pale overhead one.

## Replacing one by hand

These are ordinary PNGs, so any one of them can be replaced with a painted
version without touching code — keep the size, keep what is in the alpha, and
add the surface's numbers to `terrain.json` if it is a new one. Everything
else reads the manifest and the glob, not a list of names.
